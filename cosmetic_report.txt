1.Problem Statement and Motivation 
Cosmetic products are sold through multiple online marketplaces, and information about the same or 
similar products is often distributed across separate listings. A user who wants to identify a cosmetic 
product from its packaging may need to inspect the br and name, product type and other printed 
information manually. Comparing listings, prices and customer feedback across platforms can also 
require considerable time. 
CosmeticVerify addresses this problem by bringing image -based product identification, packaging text 
extraction and marketplace comparison into one application. The system accepts a cosmetic product 
image and uses a pretrained vision-language model together with optical character recognition to support 
identification. It then presents available marketplace information and a recommendation based on the 
information retrieved and configured analysis. 
The system is intended as a decision-support tool. A marketplace listing match or recommendation does 
not establish that a product is authentic or counterfeit. Results depend on image quality, available product 
references and the accessibility and reliability of marketplace information. 
  
2. Aim and Objective  
Aim: To develop an AI-assisted system that identifies cosmetic products from packaging images, extracts 
relevant text, compares available marketplace listings and presents an explainable recommendation to 
support user decisions.

3.Methodology and System Design 
 
 
 
Figure 1: CosmeticVerify System Architecture and Product Analysis Workflow. 
 
CosmeticVerify follows a layered architecture consisting of a Next.js frontend, Node.js backend and 
Python FastAPI AI service. It integrates pretrained CLIP, Tesseract OCR and marketplace data retrieval 
for cosmetic product identification and comparison. 
Figure 1: CosmeticVerify System Architecture and Product Analysis Workflow 
The workflow begins with image uploading, followed by CLIP -based product identification and OCR 
text extraction. The backend retrieves available marketplace information, performs comparisons and 
presents the analysis and final recommendation. 
 
3.1 AI and Matching Approach 
The system uses pretrained CLIP ViT -B/32 to extract image embeddings. Cosine similarity compares 
uploaded images with product reference images. Tesseract OCR extracts visible packaging text to support 
product identification.

5. Progress up to Mid-Term (with Evidence) 
The project has progressed to an integrated prototype consisting of a Next.js frontend, Node.js backend 
and Python-based AI service. The system demonstrates cosmetic image uploading, product identification, 
OCR, marketplace comparison and recommendation ge neration. Evidence of the implemented features 
is provided in Appendices A–F. 
5.1 Frontend Development 
The frontend provides an image-upload interface and displays product identification, verification results, 
marketplace comparisons, review analysis and recommendations. Further UI improvements are planned 
to enhance usability. 
Evidence: Appendices A and B. 
5.2 AI-Based Product Identification and OCR 
The system uses pretrained CLIP ViT -B/32 for visual product matching and Tesseract OCR to extract 
text from cosmetic packaging. These techniques support product identification using visual and textual 
information. 
Evidence: Appendix A. 
5.3 Product Matching and Model Evaluation 
Uploaded images are compared with stored product reference embeddings using cosine similarity. The 
existing evaluation report records 61.56% Top-1 accuracy and 54.97% Top -5 accuracy across 553 test 
images from 30 product classes. Further improvements are required to increase identification 
performance. 
Evidence: Appendix G. 
 
--- PAGE 6 ---
5.4 Marketplace Comparison 
The application demonstrates product comparison across Amazon, Daraz and Temu. Marketplace access 
restrictions and demonstration data may affect result availability; therefore, simulated information is not 
presented as verified live data. 
Evidence: Appendix C. 
5.5 Review Analysis and Recommendation 
The interface includes customer review analysis and final product recommendations. Current 
demonstration review values are simulated, and separate sentiment-model evaluation remains necessary. 
Evidence: Appendices D and E. 
5.6 Backend and AI Service Integration 
The Node.js backend communicates with the FastAPI AI service to process uploaded images. The AI 
service has been successfully started locally using Uvicorn on port 8001. Further testing is required to 
verify complete request processing and error handling. 
Evidence: Appendix F . 
 
6. Remaining Work Of The Full System 
 
The remaining work focuses on improving reliability, completing data persistence, simplifying the user 
experience and evaluating the AI pipeline. The sequence below is a proposed plan; it is not a record of 
completed work. 
1. Complete PostgreSQL integration and verify data persistence. 
2. Implement user registration, login, logout and secure user-specific history. 
3. Develop a workflow for identifying and managing new or unlisted products. 
4. Redesign the results page with a recommendation-first UI. 
5. Add saved products, analysis history and product catalogue management. 
6. Evaluate CLIP zero -shot and reference -based matching approaches to improve identification 
accuracy. 
7. Improve marketplace retrieval, error handling and upload processing. 
8. Integrate a Search Engine  to search for cosmetic product information and retrieve relevant product 
details. 
9. Complete end-to-end testing, usability evaluation, documentation and deployment preparation.

9.Conclusion and References 
 
The system is not yet a fully completed production application. Database persistence, user
